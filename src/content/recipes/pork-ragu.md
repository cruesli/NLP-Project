ingredients: |-
  - 1.2kg pork shoulder, boneless, cut into 4 large pieces
  - 2 tablespoons olive oil
  - 1 onion, finely diced
  - 2 carrots, finely diced
  - 2 celery stalks, finely diced
  - 4 garlic cloves, minced
  - 200ml dry white wine
  - 1 can peeled tomatoes
  - 500ml chicken stock
  - 1 small handful fresh rosemary
  - 4 bay leaves
  - 1 strip orange peel
  - Salt and black pepper
  - Pasta (pappardelle or rigatoni), to serve
  - Grated Parmesan, to serve
---
1. Sear: Pat 1.2kg pork shoulder dry and season heavily with salt and pepper. Heat 2 tablespoons olive oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. Brown the pork on all sides for about 10 minutes total. Set aside.

2. Soffritto: Lower the heat. Add 1 onion, 2 carrots and 2 celery stalks, all finely diced. Cook for 12 minutes, stirring, until soft and sweet. Add 4 garlic cloves, minced and cook for another 1 minute.

3. Deglaze: Pour in 200ml dry white wine and scrape up any browned bits from the base. Simmer for 3 minutes until mostly evaporated.

4. Build the braise: Add 2 tins crushed tomatoes, 250ml chicken stock, 1 small handful rosemary, 4 bay leaves and 1 strip orange peel. Return the pork to the pot, half-submerged in the sauce. Bring to a gentle simmer.

5. Slow-cook: Cover and cook on the lowest heat for 2.5 to 3 hours, turning the pork once or twice, until the meat falls apart with a fork.

6. Shred: Lift out the pork and shred with two forks, discarding any large pieces of fat. Return the meat to the sauce. Discard the rosemary stalks, bay leaves and orange peel. Season to taste.

7. Serve: Toss with cooked pasta and a splash of pasta water. Top with grated Parmesan.

A recipe that rewards patience — don't rush the soffritto or the braise. The orange peel is the secret weapon: it adds a subtle brightness that lifts the heavy braise without ever announcing itself. Even better the next day. Freezes well in portions.
