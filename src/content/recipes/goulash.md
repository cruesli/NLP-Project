ingredients: |-
  - 1.2kg beef chuck, cut into 4cm cubes
  - 3 tablespoons lard or neutral oil
  - 4 large onions, finely diced
  - 4 garlic cloves, minced
  - 4 tablespoons sweet Hungarian paprika
  - 1 teaspoon hot Hungarian paprika (optional)
  - 1 teaspoon caraway seeds, lightly crushed
  - 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  - 2 red bell peppers, diced
  - 2 ripe tomatoes, diced
  - 1.5 litres beef stock or water
  - 4 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into 3cm chunks
  - 2 bay leaves
  - Salt and black pepper
  - Sour cream and parsley, to serve
---
1. Brown beef: Pat 1.2kg beef cubes dry and season with salt. Heat 1 tablespoon lard in a heavy pot over high heat. Brown the beef in batches for 2 minutes per side until well crusted. Set aside.

2. Onion base: Lower the heat to medium. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons lard and 4 finely diced onions. Cook for 15 minutes, stirring often, until deeply golden and almost jammy. This is the foundation — don't rush it.

3. Bloom paprika: Pull the pot off the heat. Add 4 minced garlic cloves, 4 tablespoons sweet paprika, 1 teaspoon hot paprika and 1 teaspoon caraway. Stir for 30 seconds — paprika burns instantly on direct heat and turns bitter.

4. Build: Return to medium heat. Add 2 tablespoons tomato paste and cook for 1 minute. Add 2 diced bell peppers, 2 diced tomatoes and 2 bay leaves. Stir to combine.

5. Braise: Return the beef and any resting juices. Add 1.5 litres stock — just enough to cover. Bring to a simmer, partially cover, and cook on low for 90 minutes.

6. Add potatoes: Add 4 chopped potatoes and continue cooking, partially covered, for another 45 minutes until the beef is fork-tender and the potatoes have started to break down at the edges, thickening the broth.

7. Season: Taste, adjust salt and pepper, and discard the bay leaves.

8. Serve: Ladle into deep bowls with a spoon of sour cream and a scatter of parsley.

Hungarian paprika is non-negotiable — Spanish or generic supermarket paprika doesn't have the same depth. The slight breakdown of the potatoes thickens the stew naturally; no flour needed. Even better the next day, after a night for the flavours to settle.
