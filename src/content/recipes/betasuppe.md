title: Betasuppe with pork shank

servings: 6
prepTimeMinutes: 20
cookTimeMinutes: 180
image: /images/recipes/betasuppe.png
ingredients: |-
  - 1 pork shank, about 1kg, fresh or lightly salted
  - 2 litres water
  - 2 bay leaves
  - 1 teaspoon whole black peppercorns
  - 1 teaspoon whole allspice berries
  - 1 leek, sliced
  - 1 small rutabaga (kålrot), peeled and diced into 2cm cubes
  - 3 carrots, diced into 2cm cubes
  - 1 small celeriac, peeled and diced into 2cm cubes
  - 100g yellow split peas, rinsed
  - 4 potatoes, peeled and diced into 2cm cubes
  - Salt and black pepper
  - 1 small handful fresh parsley, chopped
  - Flatbread or rye bread, to serve
---
1. Stock: Place the 1kg pork shank in a large pot with 2 litres water, 2 bay leaves, 1 teaspoon peppercorns and 1 teaspoon allspice berries. Bring to a boil and skim off any grey foam that rises. Lower to a bare simmer.

2. Simmer the meat: Cook on low, partially covered, for 90 minutes. The meat should be tender but still attached to the bone.

3. Add split peas: Stir in 100g rinsed yellow split peas and continue simmering for 30 minutes.

4. Add root vegetables: Add 1 sliced leek, the diced rutabaga, 3 diced carrots and 1 diced celeriac. Simmer for 20 minutes.

5. Add potatoes: Add 4 diced potatoes and simmer for another 20 minutes, until everything is tender and the broth has thickened slightly from the split peas and starch.

6. Pull the meat: Lift out the pork shank. Strip the meat from the bone, discarding skin, fat and gristle, and shred or chop into bite-sized pieces. Return the meat to the pot.

7. Season: Discard the bay leaves and any whole spices that float up. Taste — salted shanks may need none, fresh ones a generous pinch. Add black pepper.

8. Serve: Ladle into deep bowls. Scatter with fresh parsley. Serve with flatbread or rye bread on the side.

A true winter soup — the kind that eats like a meal. Salted or smoked pork shank gives more depth, but a fresh shank works fine if you season more aggressively at the end. The split peas and starchy root vegetables thicken the broth naturally over time, so this is even better the next day. Freezes well in portions.
