ingredients: |-
  Pork katsu:
  - 2 pork loin steaks, about 150g each
  - 50g plain flour
  - 1 egg, beaten
  - 80g panko breadcrumbs
  - Salt and black pepper
  - Neutral oil, for frying

  Donburi:
  - 1 onion, thinly sliced
  - 200ml dashi
  - 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  - 2 tablespoons mirin
  - 1 tablespoon sugar
  - 4 eggs, lightly beaten
  - 2 spring onions, sliced
  - 2 portions hot steamed rice
---
1. Prepare cutlets: Pound 2 pork loin steaks lightly to about 1cm thick. Season with salt and pepper. Dust in 50g flour, dip in 1 beaten egg, then coat in 80g panko, pressing firmly so the crumbs stick.

2. Fry: Heat 2cm of neutral oil in a deep pan to 170°C. Fry the cutlets for 4 minutes, flipping halfway, until deep golden and cooked through. Rest on a rack and slice into 2cm strips.

3. Simmer onions: In a small frying pan, combine 200ml dashi, 3 tablespoons soy sauce, 2 tablespoons mirin and 1 tablespoon sugar. Add 1 sliced onion and simmer for 5 minutes until soft and translucent.

4. Add cutlet: Lay the sliced cutlet on top of the onions, keeping the slices together. Pour 4 lightly beaten eggs over the top in a swirl. Cover with a lid and cook for 90 seconds — the eggs should be just set with a soft, custardy centre.

5. Serve: Slide the whole thing onto a bowl of hot rice. Scatter with 2 sliced spring onions.

Don't overcook the eggs — the magic of katsudon is the just-set, almost-runny topping. Cook each portion separately for the cleanest result; doubling up in one pan makes the eggs steam unevenly and the cutlet go soggy.
